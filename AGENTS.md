# Writing educational material

When writing or revising chapters, exercises, captions and other student-facing text, use the opening paragraph of [Convolutions](book/cnn/convolution_and_pooling.md#convolutions) as a model. It starts with a familiar task, applying a filter to a signal, then names the operation, describes a single window and explains where the result goes.

Introduce a concept through the question it answers or an example students can already follow. Explain the steps in the order a student would carry them out. Make it clear what each quantity represents and how to interpret the result. A familiar example can carry the explanation from one section to the next.

Use natural sentences and introduce notation when it makes an idea easier to express. Include a technical condition where the reader needs it to interpret an example or use an equation correctly. Aim for enough detail that students can follow the reasoning on a first reading, and cut sentences that merely repeat it.
