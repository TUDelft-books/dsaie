# Introduction to Deep Learning

In this introductory lecture, we'll explore the fundamentals of deep learning, starting with the **curse of dimensionality** — the challenge of processing and learning from high-dimensional data. We'll examine how deep learning models leverage **inductive biases**, which are built-in assumptions, to interpret large datasets and identify patterns. These biases are crucial for overcoming the curse of dimensionality, enabling models to perform effectively. We'll cover the primary types of inductive biases: *spatial* biases managed by **convolutional layers**, *sequential biases* implemented by **recurrent layers**, and the *hierarchical* structure of data processed by deep neural networks. Additionally, we'll discuss how these biases can be integrated within models to improve learning. The session will also introduce other inductive biases and their corresponding layers and architectures, such as *graph neural networks* for non-Euclidean data structures, *transformers* for attention-based learning, and *Physics-Informed Neural Networks* that incorporate physical laws into machine learning. By the end of the lecture, you'll have a foundational understanding of how deep learning models operate and why they're so effective. You'll also see these concepts in action through a practical application using famous computer vision datasets.
